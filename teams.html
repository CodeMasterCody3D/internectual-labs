01 / Research & directionOwner & Founder

@CodeMasterCody3D

Cody Dixon

Independent AI researcher & builder

33 · Hueytown, Alabama

Background & journey

I’m Cody, owner and founder of Internectual Labs, an independent AI researcher and builder. I started by overclocking hardware and digging into Linux, learning through forums and trial and error. Getting RuneScape running smoothly on Android through Linux was an early win. Later, I pushed an i7-8086K with a compact 120 mm liquid cooler and tuned mining rigs for efficiency. Those experiments taught me to balance performance, power, and stability.

Technical focus

AI took me from not knowing how to code to building my own projects. Now I’m drawn to the same question that drove my hardware experiments: how much more can we get out of less? I want to shrink AI models while retaining their intelligence, connecting model efficiency with the practical limits of the hardware running them.

Work at the lab

That drive led to TAARDIS-27B, the lab’s full-ternary model project. My TK Integers work explores ternary weight formats and GPU kernels. IQ wave models are more experimental, exploring geometry and interacting waves in place of standard layers. I also build tools: GRIP helps AI-assisted projects keep track of where they left off, and GNU-AI-Flow is a visual workspace for model components and training pipelines.

Personal approach

I love pushing limits, questioning assumed boundaries, and improving things until the results hold up. When something fails, I want to understand why and keep working at it. I value friendly, honest conversation and hearing what people actually care about. The lab grows from that same curiosity: build something, test it, share what you learn, and keep going.

  • IQ wave models
  • Tools for builders

    04 / Evaluation & reliabilityNew Team Member

    @Emart29

    Emmanuel Nwanguma

    ML & MLOps Engineer

    Evaluation, LLM reliability, and QLoRA fine-tuning

    Lagos, Nigeria

    Portfolio ↗

    Background & journey

    I’m Emmanuel, an ML and MLOps engineer with a background in biochemistry and experience in data quality, analysis, and machine learning systems. That path shaped my interest in whether a measurement actually holds up. My work now spans models and the infrastructure around them: evaluation tools, feature stores, data lineage, and deployment systems. I also contribute to open source through testing, integrations, and reliability improvements.

    Technical focus

    I focus on LLM evaluation, reliability, and QLoRA fine-tuning. I’m interested in what happens beyond a successful demonstration: whether structured outputs remain correct, whether citations support an answer, and whether model updates introduce regressions. My engineering work includes Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face, FastAPI, Docker, and tools for tracking experiments and monitoring deployed models.

    Projects & planned lab work

    My projects include so-agent, a benchmark for structured-output reliability; ragwell, a retrieval system that checks quoted evidence against source passages; and MLOps tools for consistent feature serving and controlled model rollouts.

    At Internectual Labs, I want to take responsibility for building a reproducible evaluation harness for TAARDIS. My proposed starting point is to compare the ternary model against FP16 and a standard 4-bit baseline using perplexity and subsets of MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval. The aim is to measure each new quantization idea consistently, making quality changes and tradeoffs easier to identify. This is my planned contribution as I get started.

    Personal approach

    I care about evidence people can inspect and results they can reproduce. That includes checking the evaluation itself: a misleading metric or a flawed test can hide useful progress or make a weak result look convincing. I document failures and corrections alongside results, and I value honest discussion about limitations. What attracted me to Internectual Labs was the willingness to experiment openly and acknowledge where the work still needs improvement.

    • Reproducible evaluation & benchmarks
    • LLM reliability & regression testing
    • QLoRA fine-tuning & MLOps
